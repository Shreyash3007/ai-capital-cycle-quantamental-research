# Universe and segmentation

## Universe principle

The project does not use "AI company" as a loose marketing label. A company enters the research universe only when a documented part of its revenue, cost structure, capital spending, asset utilization, competitive position, or valuation is materially connected to the AI capital cycle.

The universe is versioned through time. Inclusion today does not imply inclusion historically.

## Private frontier-lab nodes

OpenAI and Anthropic remain private ecosystem nodes as of 2026-09-23. Both have announced confidential draft S-1 submissions, which create an option to pursue an IPO but do not make their securities publicly traded:

- [OpenAI confidential S-1 announcement](https://openai.com/index/openai-submits-confidential-s-1/)
- [Anthropic confidential S-1 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)

They can be studied through disclosed funding, partnerships, capacity commitments, product adoption, competitive effects, and their impact on listed suppliers or partners. They cannot yet be treated like ordinary listed equities with public return histories and complete public-company filings.

## Candidate public segments

These are research candidates, not permanent portfolio constituents or recommendations.

### Segment A: compute silicon and custom acceleration

- NVIDIA (`NVDA`)
- AMD (`AMD`)
- Broadcom (`AVGO`)

Primary economics: accelerator demand, custom silicon, networking, pricing power, product cycles, supply, and customer concentration.

### Segment B: foundry and semiconductor equipment

- Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (`TSM`)
- ASML (`ASML`)

Primary economics: leading-edge capacity, process transitions, equipment bottlenecks, utilization, geopolitical exposure, and customer capital cycles.

### Segment C: networking, power, and thermal infrastructure

- Arista Networks (`ANET`)
- Vertiv (`VRT`)

Primary economics: cluster networking, data-center power density, cooling, order conversion, backlog, capacity, and hyperscaler concentration.

### Segment D: AI-specialized cloud infrastructure

- CoreWeave (`CRWV`)

Primary economics: contracted capacity, utilization, power access, customer concentration, debt and lease obligations, accelerator supply, backlog quality, and cash requirements. CoreWeave states that it completed its Nasdaq listing in March 2025 in its [investor results](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Reports-Strong-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Results/).

### Segment E: hyperscalers and model platforms

- Microsoft (`MSFT`)
- Alphabet (`GOOGL`)
- Amazon (`AMZN`)
- Meta Platforms (`META`)
- Oracle (`ORCL`)

Primary economics: AI capex, cloud demand, internal model investment, inference monetization, depreciation, software distribution, and free-cash-flow trade-offs.

### Segment F: enterprise data and AI applications

- Palantir (`PLTR`)
- Snowflake (`SNOW`)
- ServiceNow (`NOW`)
- Salesforce (`CRM`)
- C3.ai (`AI`)
- Tempus AI (`TEM`)

Primary economics: consumption or subscription growth, AI product attachment, retention, implementation costs, gross margin, sales efficiency, stock compensation, and evidence of production adoption. Palantir, for example, describes AIP and its operating role in its [2025 Form 10-K](https://investors.palantir.com/files/2025%20FY%20PLTR%2010-K.pdf).

## Reference universes

Evaluation requires controls and peers:

- broad technology equities;
- semiconductor and equipment peers;
- cloud and enterprise-software peers;
- data-center infrastructure peers;
- market and sector indexes;
- rate, credit, liquidity, and power-cost series.

A reference company can help estimate normal behavior without being called an AI company.

## Segmentation rules

- Never pool companies merely because they mention AI.
- Use segment-specific targets and features when business models differ.
- Do not compare a foundry multiple directly with a subscription-software multiple without an explicit model.
- Preserve company-quarter panels to expand observations, but use filing availability dates.
- Control for common time effects and broad market factors when the learned methods permit it.
- Version additions, removals, ticker changes, acquisitions, and delistings.
- Keep failed, distressed, and acquired companies in historical tests to limit survivorship bias.

## Initial real-data focus

After the C1W2 synthetic foundation is demonstrated, start the frozen real-data study with the AI infrastructure and bottleneck chain: Segments A-D. Its AI economics are comparatively direct and create a coherent capital-cycle question.

Eight current companies are not enough for a reliable five-feature cross-sectional predictive model. The first real analysis must therefore be presented as descriptive or use a carefully constructed point-in-time company-quarter panel. Predictive claims wait for adequate observations and valid time-aware evaluation.

## Private-to-public exposure graph

Later, store relationships as versioned evidence rather than permanent assumptions:

```text
private lab
    -> cloud or distribution partner
    -> compute and networking supplier
    -> power and data-center provider
    -> financing provider
    -> enterprise or consumer demand channel
```

Each relationship requires a source, start date, end date if known, economic mechanism, and confidence. Partnership headlines alone do not establish revenue materiality.
