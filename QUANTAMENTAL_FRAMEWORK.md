# Quantamental framework

## Definition

This project uses **quantamental research**: fundamental analysis defines the economic mechanism and accounting meaning; quantitative methods measure, test, rank, and monitor it; machine learning models nonlinear or high-dimensional relationships; AI systems extract and evaluate textual evidence; portfolio research determines whether any signal has usable economic value.

No layer may substitute for another. A statistical relationship without an economic mechanism is fragile. A compelling company story without measurement is not a tested signal. An AI-generated explanation without source-linked evidence is not research.

## Central research question

> Across the public AI capital cycle, where is durable economic value being created, what expectations are already embedded in prices, and which companies or segments are most exposed if operational delivery, financing conditions, or market narratives change?

## The AI capital-cycle chain

```text
end-user demand and model adoption
    -> token, training, inference, and data demand
    -> cloud capacity and accelerator demand
    -> networking, memory, foundry, equipment, power, and cooling demand
    -> capex, depreciation, financing, and utilization
    -> revenue, margins, cash conversion, and return on invested capital
    -> market expectations and valuation
    -> positioning, crowding, liquidity, and price behavior
    -> catalysts, repricing, and portfolio outcomes
```

The system must model both value creation and value transfer. High spending by one segment can be revenue for another while reducing the spender's free cash flow. The same AI boom can therefore strengthen a supplier and weaken a customer's near-term capital efficiency.

## Eight research layers

### 1. Accounting and fundamental truth

Normalize revenue, gross profit, operating profit, free cash flow, capex, research spending, stock compensation, dilution, debt, cash, backlog, and customer concentration. Every metric must define its source, period, unit, availability date, and transformation.

Core questions:

- Is growth organic, acquired, price-driven, volume-driven, or accounting-driven?
- Is margin expansion operational or caused by mix and classification?
- Does free cash flow survive stock compensation, working-capital timing, and required capex?
- Is backlog firm, cancellable, financed, or capacity-constrained?
- Does incremental investment earn an acceptable return?

### 2. AI unit economics and industry structure

Study the economic engine behind the statements:

- training versus inference demand;
- cost per token or workload where evidence permits;
- accelerator utilization and useful life;
- depreciation assumptions;
- power, cooling, networking, and memory bottlenecks;
- customer concentration and supplier dependence;
- software gross margins versus infrastructure intensity;
- pricing power, switching costs, and ecosystem control;
- build-versus-buy choices among labs, clouds, and enterprises.

### 3. Valuation and embedded expectations

Use multiples only after denominator quality is established. Combine peer-relative valuation with reverse valuation methods that ask what growth, margin, capital intensity, and duration the current price appears to require.

Outputs include:

- normalized valuation multiples;
- model-estimated valuation and residual stretch;
- implied growth and margin paths;
- sensitivity to discount rates and terminal assumptions;
- downside and upside scenario ranges;
- the specific assumptions that dominate value.

### 4. Quantitative market state

Measure returns, residual momentum, volatility, drawdown, liquidity, turnover, dispersion, correlation, crowding proxies, and price explosiveness. Market data describes behavior and positioning; it does not prove the fundamental cause.

### 5. Statistical and machine-learning models

Use the least complex method that answers the question:

- cross-sectional and panel regression;
- regularized regression and classification;
- event studies;
- tree ensembles for nonlinear interactions;
- clustering and anomaly detection for regimes;
- calibrated event-probability models;
- sequence and deep-learning models only after simpler time-series baselines.

Every model requires point-in-time inputs, a baseline, walk-forward evaluation, stability analysis, and an economic interpretation.

### 6. AI evidence layer

Use TypeSafe/Jev for narrow typed judgments over exact filing or transcript excerpts. Use language models for constrained extraction, comparison, and explanation. Numeric calculations, weights, portfolio rules, and final high-stakes conclusions remain in deterministic code or human review.

Candidate semantic measurements include monetization-evidence strength, claim specificity, capacity-constraint evidence, competitive-risk change, and narrative-to-fundamental divergence.

### 7. Catalyst and fragility layer

Connect present conditions to conditional pathways rather than exact crash dates. Study earnings delivery, capacity additions, financing needs, customer loss, rate shocks, regulation, supply normalization, and crowded-position unwinds.

### 8. Portfolio and risk layer

A useful company signal is not automatically a useful portfolio. Test exposure, diversification, turnover, costs, borrow assumptions, liquidity, beta, sector factors, concentration, drawdown, and capacity. Real-money execution remains outside scope unless separately authorized.

## Signal families

The mature engine maintains components before composites:

| Signal family | Question answered |
|---|---|
| Fundamental Delivery | Are reported operating results catching up with prior expectations? |
| AI Revenue Quality | Is AI-linked growth realized, repeatable, diversified, and cash-generative? |
| Capital Efficiency | What revenue, profit, and cash-flow improvement follows incremental investment? |
| Expectations Gap | What operating path appears embedded in the current valuation? |
| Valuation Stretch | How far is observed valuation from a transparent conditional model? |
| Narrative Evidence Gap | How far do management claims exceed disclosed operational evidence? |
| Crowding and Price Fragility | How vulnerable is price behavior to a reversal in positioning or liquidity? |
| Funding Fragility | How dependent is the business or ecosystem on refinancing, dilution, or abundant capital? |
| Catalyst State | Which observable events could validate or break the thesis? |

## Confidence is a vector

Never collapse uncertainty prematurely. Preserve:

- **data confidence:** completeness, freshness, provenance, and accounting comparability;
- **model confidence:** out-of-sample error, stability, calibration, and regime dependence;
- **semantic confidence:** labeled-set accuracy, probability distribution, and evidence coverage;
- **economic confidence:** strength of the proposed mechanism and alternative explanations;
- **timing confidence:** uncertainty about horizon and catalyst path;
- **portfolio confidence:** sensitivity to costs, exposures, liquidity, and construction choices.

## Research hierarchy

Outputs must say which level they occupy:

1. **Measurement:** what is true in the recorded data?
2. **Diagnosis:** what mechanism may explain it?
3. **Ranking:** which observations are relatively stronger or weaker?
4. **Forecast:** what defined future outcome is estimated over what horizon?
5. **Scenario:** what happens if declared assumptions change?
6. **Allocation research:** how would a versioned rule behave in a paper portfolio?

Moving upward requires stronger evidence. A measurement must not be presented as a forecast, and a ranking must not be presented as an executable trade.
